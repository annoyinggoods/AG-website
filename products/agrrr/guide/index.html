Start here: your first website, click by click

This guide assumes you have never built a website and want every click spelled out. Nothing here needs code, an account, or money. You need a Windows computer, the free AGrrr! installer, and about half an hour. Every screenshot below is the app in its Hallowe'en look — yours may wear a different theme, and every button is where the pictures show it.

Step 1 — Install and open AGrrr!

Go to the download page, save AGRr!-Setup-0.9.0.exe, and double-click it. The installer asks nothing clever — keep the answers it offers. When it finishes, open AGRr! from your Start menu, like any other program.

The first thing you see is the welcome card. Read its three lines if you like — they are the whole product in miniature — and then press Start building.

Step 2 — Meet the window, once

One tour, top to bottom, and you will never need it again:

Top-left: the menu bar — File (save a version, export, version history, settings) and View (screen sizes for the canvas). Top strip: the toolbar — Select (your normal mode), Undo and Redo, then doors to Responsive, Chat, Settings and About.

Page bar: under the toolbar it says Untitled page · Home with a + Add page button at the right end — your site's pages live in this strip.

Left panel — the building blocks. Its tabs are, in order: Elements (blank blocks you place yourself), Library (more than 1,200 ready-made blocks), Layers (the tree of everything on the page), Theme (the site's fonts and colours), Assets (your pictures and fonts).

Middle: the canvas — your real page, rendered live.

Right panel — the inspector. Its tabs: Properties (everything about the thing you selected), Backend, Deploy, Code, Chat, Responsive, Contrast report, Dependencies. On day one you will only use Properties.

Bottom edge: a little line that says Autosaved to disk. — the app saves your work by itself, constantly. Closing the app loses nothing.

Your first block, and your first words

Step 3 — Place a Text block (one click)

In the Elements tab you will see the blocks: Free box · DIV, Section, Navigation, Header, Footer, Main content, Text, Button, Image, List, Form, Divider, Icon, Video, Link.

Find Text and press it — one press, no dragging needed. A text block appears on the page, already selected, and the right panel fills with its details.

Step 4 — Type your words into it

Look at the right panel, on the Properties tab. Two fields matter right now:

Under Identity, the Alias field — this is the block's name for you, not for visitors. Type something you will recognise later, like Intro line. Under Content, the Text field — type the sentence your visitors should read. The canvas updates the moment you type.

Step 5 — A section, a button, a list

Back in Elements, press Section (a container that will hold a group of things), then Button, then List. Each press places one.

To fill the button in, click it on the canvas first — selecting a block is always just a click. In Properties, its Content section has a Label field: type the button's text, for example See what it makes. Click the list, and fill Items, one per line — press Enter between items.

Ready-made blocks: the Library

Step 6 — Search the Library

Hand-placed blocks are for simple pages. For the impressive parts — heroes, galleries, contact sections, menus, effects — open the Library tab in the left panel. Type into Search snippets: try hero, or contact form, or gallery. Every result is a finished, tested piece of website with gaps left for your own words.

Step 7 — Look before you take

Press a result row and the Library opens the block's detail: its Parameters — the gaps you will fill, like a headline or a picture address — and a live Preview of the block as it renders. Close it with the close button when you have seen enough.

Step 8 — Drag it onto the page

Press and hold on a result card, drag it over the canvas, and do not let go yet. As you move over the page, AGrrr! shows exactly where the block will land: a band that reads Nest inside means it goes inside the block under your pointer; Insert before or Insert after means it becomes a neighbour of that block. Let go, and it is on your page.

Some library blocks need a small helper piece to work — a font, or a companion block. If so, an offer appears asking to bring it in; accept, and both pieces arrive together.

Arranging, renaming, and the safety nets

Step 9 — One block floats: the Free box

Every block you press lands in the page's flow, one after another, the way a document reads. One block is different: Free box · DIV floats on the page. With a free box selected, hold Ctrl and drag any of its edges to move it anywhere; drag its corner grip to resize it. Everything else keeps flowing around it.

For every other block, arranging means order: select a block and use its ⋯ menu to move it, or drag rows inside the Layers tab.

Step 10 — Find and rename anything in Layers

As the page grows, clicking tiny things on the canvas gets fiddly. The Layers tab lists every element on the page as a row, in tree order, with the alias you gave it. Click a row to select that element on the canvas; each row's ⋯ menu carries Rename…, Duplicate and Delete — deleting shows a toast with an Undo button, so nothing is ever lost to a slip.

Step 11 — Zoom, and undo without fear

Above the canvas, the Zoom select runs from 25% to 400% — zoom out to judge the whole page, in to fix a detail. The toolbar's Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back any mistake, one step per press, and Redo gives it back. The app autosaves as you work — the footer says so — so a crash or a closed window costs you nothing.

Make it yours, once

Step 12 — The Theme tab dresses every page

The Theme tab in the left panel holds the site's design tokens — the colours, fonts and shapes every page shares. Change a token here and the change reaches every page and every block that uses it, without you visiting each one. You never have to touch this on day one: the default look is already coherent.

Step 13 — More pages, navigation included

A site is more than one page. Press + Add page in the page bar, and a new blank page arrives. Each page's ⋯ menu renames it, sets its address (its route), or makes it the site's home. The site's menus are derived from the pages that exist — there is no menu to hand-wire anywhere.

Ship it: export, then put it online

Step 14 — Export real files

Open the File menu and press Export… The export panel reads Gate passed when your page is ready to leave the app — if something needs deciding first (a small-screen behaviour, say), the panel says so in a sentence and the Responsive tab is where you settle it.

Type a folder into Destination folder (or press Choose folder…), then press Export page for just this page or Export site for all of them. You get plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, plus the files a host expects — and a line that opens the result in your browser so you can see it live.

Step 15 — Put it on your hosting

If you have hosting — the plan that comes with your domain — the Deploy tab in the right panel uploads the site for you. Press Add deploy target and fill the fields your host's dashboard lists: Protocol (FTP or SFTP — prefer SFTP when your host offers it), Host, Port, Username, Remote path and Password. The password goes into your computer's own keychain, never into the project.

Press Test connection first; when it answers, Deploy to … uploads the site and reports every file it wrote. The site on your host is the same bytes the export wrote.

When something goes wrong

AGrrr! refuses things in words, never in silence: if a placement cannot work, a sentence tells you why and what to do instead. Undo takes back any step. Autosave keeps the work on disk as you go, and File → Version history… keeps named snapshots you can always go back to. Nothing you do in this guide can break the computer, the internet, or even the page for long.
