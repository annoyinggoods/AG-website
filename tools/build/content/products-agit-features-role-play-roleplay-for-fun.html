Roleplay for fun

Sometimes the writing brain needs the evening off, and the play brain wants the wheel. The fun side of role-play is the same engine with no manuscript attached: set a scene, pick who is in it, and let it run. No word counts, no goals, no draft quietly judging you from the sidebar.

It is the same AI layer you configured — your keys, or a local model — so the hobby costs exactly what you already decided it should cost.

Evenings this is good for

A few honest uses

  • The solo campaign your tabletop group keeps rescheduling: the spider dungeon-masters, you roll the dice.
  • Improv warm-ups: five minutes of scene work before writing, to get the dialogue ear in.
  • Character studies for their own sake: the supporting cast you will never write a book about, but enjoy anyway.
  • Pure nonsense, which after a hard drafting week is a legitimate literary genre.

Kept apart, on purpose

Play never wears the work clothes

Fun sessions run in their own space, not inside your novel's project. The transcript stays in the playpen; nothing syncs into the manuscript, and the compendium does not learn the dragon's name by accident. When you go back to the book, the book is undisturbed.

If the play turns out to be research after all, copy the good parts across yourself — the writing side of role-play is the door built for that.
