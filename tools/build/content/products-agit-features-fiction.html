Fiction, for the long haul

A novel is a promise made in chapter one and kept in chapter thirty. AGIT's fiction mode exists so you stop paying the remembering tax: the compendium holds the characters, locations, items and plot points; the index has read every scene you wrote; and the outline keeps the whole architecture visible while you mess with one brick.

It is not AI ghostwriting your book. It is the studio around the book — and when you do bring the AI in, it answers from your project instead of guessing.

The compendium: a story bible that fills itself in

Characters, locations, items — without the binder

Every project carries a compendium: the cast, the map, the artefacts, the lore. You can fill it by hand like any story bible, but AGIT can also suggest entries from the scenes you have already written — the side character who accidentally became important gets caught, named and filed.

In the editor, compendium entries become live links in your prose. Hover to recall who this person is; click to jump to their card. When you mention the tavern again in chapter twenty, the studio remembers who owns it, even if you do not.

Anything the AI writes consults the compendium first, and the compendium itself is never silently rewritten — suggestions wait for your approval. The bible is yours; the studio just takes minutes.

Drafting: scenes, snapshots and the long middle

Scene by scene, without fear

Books break into chapters, chapters into scenes, and each scene drafts in the editor with autosave and snapshots standing behind it. Wrote yourself into a corner? Yesterday's version of the scene is two clicks away, and the version before that too.

Writing goals and word counts track progress without nagging. A distraction-free mode strips the desk down to the page for the days the deadline bites. And when a scene refuses to work, ask the chat for three variations — with the surrounding chapters in its context, so the variations fit the book you actually wrote.

Continuity, checked while you sleep-write

The quiet superpower: the local index. It has read your draft, your notes and your research, so continuity questions become lookups instead of archaeology. "Did I already describe the harbour?" "Which hand did Cayster lose?" Ask in the chat; get the scene reference back.

It all runs on your machine, against files on your machine. A series bible that spans four books fits in the same web.

Structure: the outline and the boards

The outline forgives replotters

Drag a chapter from act one to act three and the project tree follows. Word counts and draft status sit next to every scene, so the sagging middle is visible before an editor points at it. The plan is a tool, not a contract — reorder ruthlessly.

The boards hold the mess

Every project ships with boards for the things that are not prose yet: a timeline canvas, a relationship map, a lore canvas, and free-form card walls for clippings and questions. When the plot threads tangle, spread them out on the wall and untangle in the open.

Interview your characters before they derail chapter nine, or head to the download and start the draft you have been outlining in your head.
