Roleplay for writing

The hardest scenes to write are the ones where you are not sure the character would really say it. Rehearsal solves that: put the character in the room, ask the hostile question, and hear what comes back — in their voice, from their motives, with their memory of the plot.

Nothing typed in a rehearsal lands in the manuscript. The output is understanding, and the transcript is there when you want to mine it for dialogue.

How the rehearsal is staged

Voice profiles

Each character card can carry a voice: how they speak, what they won't say, what they want and what they fear. The role-play engine reads the profile plus the compendium entry plus what has actually happened in the draft — so in chapter twelve's rehearsal, the character does not know what you plan for chapter twenty, and cannot spoil it.

Scene setting

You set the stage: the location, who else is present, the tension level, the thing that must not be said. Then you step in as the counterparty — the detective, the betraying friend, the editor from hell — and the scene runs. End it whenever the question is answered.

What writers actually rehearse

  • The confrontation: does the antagonist's argument hold up, or did you write a straw man you will have to fix in revision?
  • The interview: interrogate your own protagonist about the plot hole you are ignoring. They will find it. They live there.
  • The dialogue test: two characters, one topic — and you learn their voices are interchangeable two chapters before a reader would have told you.
  • The world check: ask a scholar character how the magic system works. If the answer contradicts chapter four, better now than in the reviews.

After the rehearsal

From transcript to draft

A good line from a rehearsal is yours to keep: copy it into the scene, or keep the transcript as a note on the character's card. When you are done rehearsing, the manuscript is exactly where you left it — untouched, because play belongs to the chat panel, not the page.

The other door, for when the manuscript needs a break instead: roleplay for fun.
