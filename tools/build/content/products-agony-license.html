License

AGony is open source, with ethics attached

AGony, the desktop environment that lives in a terminal, is free and open-source software under the Annoying Goods Open Source License with Ethical Restrictions — a Hippocratic-based license. In plain terms:

You may use it at home, use it at work, sell things you make with it, copy it, change it and share your changed copies — commercially or not. The license travels with every copy: keep the license text and the notices, and say what you changed. The ethical restrictions stay: the software may not be used for the harms the license lists, among them violence, slavery, torture, discrimination, mass surveillance, and ecocide. AGony is a reserved name, so modified versions must be renamed so that no confusion with the original is possible. It comes with no warranty: the software is offered as is, and neither the studio nor the contributors are liable for what it does on your machine.

It is not OSI-open-source — the ethical restrictions are the point. The full license text follows on this page, it ships inside the download archive as LICENSE, and the canonical text lives in the project's repository. If the plain-English summary above and the license text ever disagree, the license text wins.

What AGony stands on

Standing credits

AGony is a thin, opinionated layer over other people's good work, and the credits are built into the product: the first run lands a THANK-YOU.md on the AGony desktop listing every dependency with its license, and agony thanks regenerates it any time. The standing ones:

  • rmux, rmux-sdk and ratatui-rmux (MIT OR Apache-2.0) — the multiplexer engine AGony lives on. AGony never touches a terminal itself; the engine owns the plumbing and the process tree.
  • ratatui (MIT) — the Rust toolkit that draws every widget — and crossterm (MIT), which reads your keys.
  • carapace-bin (MIT) — the completion engine behind the command bar's suggestions, driven as a subprocess with a timeout, never inline.
  • ignore (Unlicense OR MIT) — ripgrep's walker, giving the file panel git's exact ignore semantics.
  • tokio, serde, clap, chrono, sha2, thiserror and their own dependencies — the ordinary machinery, each credited in the generated THANK-YOU.md with its exact version and license.

The curated app catalog names the license of every program it offers, and copyleft programs are never bundled into AGony's own download — they install through their own recipes, with their license in the open.
