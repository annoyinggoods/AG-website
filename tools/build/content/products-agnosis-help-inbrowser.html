AGnosis in the browser — the short guide

This page is for the browser version of AGnosis — the parade and the duck editor that run on the playground page with nothing to install. If you want the desktop app (the hotkey that hides your browsers), read the desktop guide instead. Everything here is "press this, this happens" — no jargon, no steps you can skip.

Watch and poke the parade

Start it

Click "Click to start the parade". The ducks walk in from the left, two big ones first, nine small ones behind, balloons on ropes over their backs. The whole parade is running in your browser — nothing is downloaded to your computer and nothing is sent anywhere.

Poke a duck

Click a duck
It jumps and clucks, and keeps marching.
Shift-click a duck
It stops in place. The ducks behind it stop too; the ones in front keep walking. Shift-click again (or click it) and it rejoins the line.
pause
Freezes the parade. The button turns into resume.

Bring your own art

Click art (.agnosis / manifest) and pick a file. A .agnosis package (what the desktop app's agnosis export writes) or a manifest .json both work: your drawn ducks replace the built-in ones while the parade keeps running. Type this, this happens: pick the file → the ducks change immediately. Nothing is uploaded; the file never leaves the page.

Draw your own duck in the editor

Open it

Click "open the editor". The parade pauses and the editor takes its place: a black canvas, the tool strip on the right, and a top bar. Press ESC (or click "back to the parade") to go back — your drawing stays until you close the tab.

Draw

Click a cell to paint it; press and drag to paint many cells in one go. Pick one of the 19 tools (the glyphs in the right strip) and one of the 8 colours (the coloured squares at the bottom of the strip). The drawing goes into the frame of a big duck first; the top bar decides what you are drawing.

The top bar, left to right: what kind (BAL balloon, SPR walking duck, GRS grass tile, DEC hat), which size (BIG/SML), how many frames (1 still, up to 4 poses), and the name field. Click a bar, click an option in the list that opens. The name already carries a free suggestion — sprite-1 — and your first typed letter replaces it.

Press this, this happens

Click on the canvas
Paints one cell (drag = many cells, one undo step).
Enter
Saves — with more than one frame chosen, it first stores the pose and shows the previous one faintly for the next.
Ctrl+Z
Undoes the last stroke. Ctrl+Shift+Z (or Ctrl+Y) redoes.
F1
The settings panel: sliders, toggles and dropdowns for the parade's look. A change applies to the parade on the spot.
F2
The assign panel: pick a parade slot on the left, pick one of your drawn assets on the right — your art replaces that duck.
F4
Back to the drawing canvas from any panel.
Arrow keys
Scroll the canvas (and step values in the settings panel).
Escape
Closes an open list first, then leaves a panel, then returns to the parade.

Save (and where it lands)

Click SAVE (or press Enter). The document is saved inside the page — the parade can use it right away — and a copy lands in your browser's downloads as sprite-1.json (or the name you typed). That download is your only copy: the browser tab does not keep files, so keep the downloads. Put a downloaded document into the desktop app's drawn folder, or send a whole set around with export art (.agnosis).

Note the two things the browser editor does not have yet: the note strip (TRK answers politely — it is a desktop feature for now) and sound. Undo, drafts and everything else about drawing work as they do on the desktop, minus the autosave: on the web, saving means downloading.

Take the parade with you

Three buttons in the bar, each writes a file into your downloads. The recording runs on the page — no server, no upload.

export page (.html)
One self-contained page that replays your parade anywhere — open it straight from the downloads folder, send it to a friend.
export GIF (.gif)
The same loop as an animated GIF.
export art (.agnosis)
A package of everything you drew or imported — the desktop app imports it back with agnosis import.

Three small questions

Is anything uploaded or installed?

No. The parade runs inside the page; your art files are read in the browser and your exports are written by the browser. No account, no server, no install.

Where does my art go when I close the tab?

Into your downloads, if you saved it. The page itself keeps nothing — there is no disk in a browser tab — so a saved document is a downloaded document. Draw it, SAVE it, keep the download.

Why is it silent?

The browser version has no sound yet — the ducks march silently on purpose for now. The desktop app synthesizes every sound itself.
