The writing desk that remembers your story

AGIT is our flagship writing environment for long projects: novels, series, scripts, essays, research. The draft, the notes, the story bible and the AI help all live in one workspace, on your own machine.

One nest for the whole project

Every long project scatters. The manuscript lives in one program, the research in fifteen browser tabs, and the helpful chatbot forgets your protagonist's name between messages.

AGIT pulls it all into one web. The chat knows your project because it reads from it. The editor autosaves as you type. The story bible suggests entries while you write, and never writes over you.

The result is a desk where the boring parts are handled and the strange parts stay yours.

Four ways into the same web

Every project picks a lane, and the workspace shapes itself around the job. The lanes share one engine, so you can cross over whenever the work does.

Fiction, for the long haul

Novels and series drown in their own details. AGIT keeps characters, locations, items and plot points in a living compendium, and can suggest entries from the scenes you have already written.

Draft scene by scene, ask for variations on a chapter, and let the compendium do the remembering. See what fiction mode does.

Non-fiction that keeps its spine

Essays, guides and documentation need structure more than encouragement. The outline holds the argument, the boards hold the clippings, the editor holds the prose.

Ask the chat to poke holes in the structure, then drag chapters around until the thing stands. See non-fiction mode.

Research with a local index

Import documents and notes, index them on your machine, and search them instantly. Run computations over your data and chart what matters without shipping a single source anywhere.

External tools plug in through MCP when you want them and stay unplugged when you do not. See research mode.

Role-play, for work and for fun

Interview your own characters before they derail chapter nine. Give each one a voice profile, set the scene, and let the dialogue run.

Or forget the deadline for an evening and let the spider run the dungeon. See role-play mode.

Your files, your machine

This is the studio's core promise, and AGIT takes it literally.

The web stays in your house

AGIT runs locally. Projects, compendium and research sit in a database on your own disk, with autosave and snapshots keeping the draft safe.

Optional sync runs through your own Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive account, encrypted before it leaves the machine.

Telemetry is opt-in, asked once on first run, and quiet after that. No advertising trackers, no social pixels. Read the privacy policy.

AI on your terms

Run local models through Ollama and keep the whole workflow on your machine, or bring your own API keys for cloud providers. You pick the models per task, and usage tracking keeps the bill visible.

Cloud models stay off until you configure them. Drafts export to plain formats, so nothing you write is stuck in the nest. See how external tools plug in.

A taste of the workspace

Chat, editor, outline, boards

Chat holds the conversation, the editor holds the prose, the outline holds the structure, and the boards hold the mess in between.

Chat
Brainstorm, critique, rewrite, role-play, with your project's context riding along.
Editor
Scene-based drafting with autosave, snapshots, and a distraction-free mode for when the deadline bites.
Outline

Boards

Around the four panels live the extras: brainstorm and critic corners, writing goals and word counts, eleven themes from reading room to hacker terminal, an audiobook exporter with local voices, and an interface translated into twenty languages.

Where AGIT stands

Honest status

AGIT 1.0.1 is the first public release, freshly out of the nest. It is a free desktop app for Windows: free to use for any purpose, your writing stays yours, and the installer may be shared freely.

The app is young and moving fast, and it is built like it matters. The codebase carries a regression suite of more than twelve thousand automated tests, because losing a manuscript is the one bug we refuse to ship.

When something breaks anyway, we want to know. Report a bug and it lands on the workbench.
